01 / Judgment

Proceed to a controlled paid pilot.
Do not scale the broad proposition yet.

There is enough implemented substance to test a business. There is not enough evidence to say the business will succeed.

Why it could work

The app connects an athlete’s assessment, coaching decision, training participation and later review. That can support a recurring academy service: a clear plan for the coach and understandable progress for the family.

The strongest potential value is helping the academy deliver consistent coaching and explain development. Better retention, more referrals and time savings are hypotheses to measure, not demonstrated outcomes.

Why it could fail

The product spans academy administration, performance science, video analysis and tournament operations. Each category has established alternatives. Coaches may see extra data entry; owners may expect fees and communications; advanced measurement may create expectations the evidence cannot support.

A polished website and many routes do not establish willingness to pay, repeat use or reliable operation.

My recommendation: sell one repeatable academy development cycle, support five paying or paid-conversion pilots in one accessible city, and decide from renewal and usage. Pause broad feature expansion while testing this. Recruit and demonstrate now; address the real-data pilot blockers below before onboarding customer records.

A real sport market.
An unmeasured software market.

The more useful commercial signal

Vendors already sell academy software with payments, parent access, attendance and reports. Sportzy publishes a badminton-specific customer case study. These indicate an existing category and competition, not independently verified market share or guaranteed demand for ChampionSpace. Sportzy customer story.

No defensible national TAM is established in this report. Public evidence reviewed does not establish the number of qualified Indian badminton academies, their annual software spend or their willingness to buy an additional performance tool. Multiplying fans by a subscription price would be misleading.

Build a market estimate from the bottom up: enumerate academies in one city → verify paying athlete count and buyer → identify a recurring problem → confirm budget → measure conversion. A proposed first lead list is 50 qualified academies; that is a research target, not a claim about market size.

05 / Initial customer hypothesis

One buyer. One daily user.
A clear family benefit.

Start here

A privately run badminton academy with approximately 50–200 regular athletes, more than one coach, junior development batches and a head coach willing to standardize reviews. These are proposed qualification criteria, not a researched segment count.

Buyer: owner/director. Internal champion: head coach. Daily users: coaches and an authorized assessment operator. Beneficiaries: athletes and parents.

Deprioritize initially

  • Tiny recreational groups with little need for formal progress review.
  • Court-rental businesses whose main problem is empty slots.
  • Elite institutes demanding validated biomechanics and integrations from day one.
  • Large chains requiring finance, procurement and migration depth immediately.

Choose the first city based on your access to owners and ability to provide support. Nearby academies and trusted coaching relationships are more valuable for this test than a national advertising campaign.

Proposed promise: “Know what each player should work on, organize that practice, and show parents what changed.”
Do not promise improved rankings, injury prevention or a specific performance gain without supporting evidence.
06 / The core offer

Sell the development cycle.

  1. 01 · BASELINEAssess a batch

    Use suitable tests with clear instructions and evidence limits.

  2. 02 · DECIDEChoose a focus

    The coach approves one meaningful development priority.

  3. 03 · PRACTISEPlan and record

    Carry the focus into sessions and record participation.

  4. 04 · REVIEWRepeat and interpret

    Use comparable conditions; distinguish change from uncertainty.

  5. 05 · EXPLAINShare the next step

    Give the athlete and parent a clear, approved summary.

Then return to the next training cycle. A measured change does not by itself prove that the software caused it.

Core academy package

Roster + practical assessments + coaching focus + weekly planning + fast attendance + review/report + scoped family access.

Operate alongside the academy’s existing payments and discovery tools initially. Include assisted onboarding and a defined support allowance.

Optional or separate

Smash Lab: deeper review after footage quality and reliability are established.

Tournament Space: separate event offer with its own support scope.

Research: specialist capability after routine adoption is proven.

In a small academy, the same person may coach and conduct assessments. The current role rules separate these actions. Design appropriate scoped capabilities; do not solve adoption by granting everyone administrator access.

09 / Explicit assumptions

Can the numbers support a business?

Price-test ₹2,500–₹5,000 per academy per month for a bounded core package, with staff and family access included. This is a proposed experiment, not validated willingness to pay or a recommended final tariff. Define athlete limits, onboarding and video allowance. Charge separately for substantial services or expensive analysis only after measuring delivery cost.

Use a documented paid pilot, potentially creditable against the first subscription. Invoice it manually if needed; automatic SaaS billing is not required to discover whether an owner will pay. A performance add-on has to justify a second budget line.

Illustrative scaleAssumed average monthly priceMonthly recurring revenueAnnualized revenue
10 academies₹2,500₹25,000₹3 lakh
50 academies₹3,500₹1.75 lakh₹21 lakh
200 academies₹5,000₹10 lakh₹1.2 crore

Scenario arithmetic only. No claim that these customer counts are available or achievable. Annualized means 12 × monthly revenue, not a first-year forecast. Revenue excludes GST, setup services and refunds; it is not profit.

Explore the operating assumptions

Edit the inputs. Nothing is sent or saved. Video compute/storage, messaging, support and onboarding can change costs materially.

Monthly revenue₹1,75,000
Contribution after variable costs₹1,25,000
After assumed fixed costs−₹75,000
Break-even paying academies80

Contribution = academies × (price − variable cost). Break-even = fixed costs ÷ per-academy contribution, rounded up. Fixed-cost input should include salaries, sales and administration. This model omits taxes, capital costs, financing, churn and cash timing. Defaults are planning assumptions, not measured company costs.

What this means: a small group of pilots proves interest, not sustainability. At the defaults, 50 customers still leave a ₹75,000 monthly shortfall. Keep support effort bounded, measure acquisition cost and avoid promising unlimited video or custom work at a low subscription price.

The following are proposed decision thresholds, not industry benchmarks. A five-academy pilot is directional business evidence, not a statistically representative market or sports-science study.

Continue

At least 3 of 5 academies pay for continuation; the designated coach uses the workflow in at least 3 of the last 4 weeks; at least 70% of the agreed eligible cohort completes the review cycle.

Improve before scaling

Owners value the output but need frequent help. Target 30% less report-preparation time against a measured baseline and no more than 2 support hours per academy/month after initial onboarding.

Stop this positioning

Fewer than 2 of 5 will continue paying, or coaches consistently regard it as extra work. Two renewals is inconclusive: narrow the segment and retest before expansion.

Questions that reveal buying intent
  • Show me how you currently prepare and explain a player’s progress.
  • Which part takes time, causes mistakes or creates parent complaints?
  • What software have you paid for, and why did you stop using any of it?
  • Would you pay the proposed pilot price now? What would block approval?
  • Who will maintain the data, and what existing task will this replace?
  • After a completed cycle, what would make you renew without a discount?
11 / Other directions

There are useful alternatives—but choose deliberately.

DirectionWhen it makes senseTrade-off and recommendation
Managed progress-review serviceAcademies want better reviews but do not have the staff/time to run the process.Offer assisted assessment/report setup with qualified practitioners. Faster learning, but service-heavy delivery and margins; do not count service work as scalable software adoption.
Academy operations productPilots repeatedly prioritize fees, reminders and parent administration above development insights.Integrate or narrowly add these workflows. A full ERP competes against broader low-priced tools; pursue only with a clear buyer advantage.
Tournament operations businessYou already have organizer relationships and can provide dependable event-day support.Run an independent paid-event test. Revenue may be episodic and support time-critical; keep it out of the academy validation score.
Specialist video productCoaches show willingness to pay for a specific badminton review task and accuracy is independently established.High validation and usability burden, plus paid and free substitutes. Keep it optional for the first academy offer.

Preferred sequence: focused academy progress workflow → paid renewals → remove the most common adoption blocker → deepen one proven advantage. Expand sports, research or marketplace features only after that sequence works.
